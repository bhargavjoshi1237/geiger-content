# Unbundle the feed's single engagement signal

Geiger Content can offer a strong set of feed-behaviour controls, and about half of them already exist inside `lib/feed`. The engine already handles short-term vs long-term taste, decay-based drift adaptation, Thompson-sampling topic bandits, exploration/bridge/fresh slots, anti-circling diversity rules and per-item explanations. Only one of its ~40 parameters, **recency**, is exposed to anyone ([live.mjs:44-56](../lib/feed/live.mjs); [engine.mjs:68-76](../lib/feed/engine.mjs)). The structural gap is that every event collapses into a single `(success, weight)` pair ([engine.mjs:212-225](../lib/feed/engine.mjs)), and that pair updates both the topic tree and the taste vectors. The feed therefore cannot tell "I want more of this topic" (interest) from "I enjoyed this item" (liking), from "this amazed / disgusted me" (emotion), or from "I already know this" (knowledge). The research case for separating them is strong. Engagement-ranked timelines raise reader anger by **+0.27 SD** while users' stated wish to see such content is **0.18 SD lower** for political posts ([Milli et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.16941v6)). Facebook cut the "angry" reaction weight to **zero** after it correlated with misinformation and toxicity ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/emoji-reactions-cute-addition-facebook-became-headache-rcna3747)). Experts actively reject beginner material, a pattern known as the expertise reversal effect ([UNSW](https://unsworks.unsw.edu.au/entities/publication/2222917b-75b2-411f-b259-8a79dff5a22f/full)). The recommended path has three tiers. First, document what already works. Second, ship quick wins that only widen `cleanSettings` and the insights panel. Third, build four new things: a reaction schema that routes each dimension to its own state variable, an emotion profile, a per-subject knowledge (Elo) model, and an explicit drift and rabbit-hole governor. Per-topic knowledge depth is genuine whitespace. The research notes found no consumer feed that exposes a depth control. The engine's dormant "frontier" machinery is ready to host one, but the active `entertainment-v2` taxonomy has flattened depth to 1, so that machinery currently does nothing.

## The engine learns drift and explores, but one scalar feeds everything

The live shorts feed is a compact stack. `FeedShortsScreen` and `use_live_feed.js` capture behaviour, `/api/feed/[operation]` routes it, and `lib/feed/live.mjs` combines a pure ranking engine, an embedding taste model and pgvector neighbours on a separate Postgres database whose DDL lives in `postgres/migrations/`. The workspace "Recommendations", "Diversity" and "Bandits" screens run on CMS entries and do not drive the shorts feed. The one exception is Feed Lab, which replays the same engine against simulated readers ([recommendations/feed_lab.jsx:42-66](../components/internal/screens/recommendations/feed_lab.jsx); [experiments/bandits.jsx:26-37](../components/internal/screens/experiments/bandits.jsx)). Any "offering" must therefore be wired through `lib/feed/*`, not those demo screens.

The profile already runs at two speeds, which is the core requirement for handling drifting interest. The first speed is the topic tree, where each node holds a Beta posterior shrunk toward the reader's own global rate and expressed as relative `lift`. It decays with a **5-day half-life** and a per-event forgetting factor of **0.994**, which the code describes as "so a shift in taste shows up within a session, not days later" ([engine.mjs:34-44, 182-210](../lib/feed/engine.mjs)). The second speed is a set of embedding channels: up to **8 per-topic long-term vectors (14-day half-life)**, a **45-minute "right now" vector**, and a **3-day negative vector** built from hides and skips ([taste.mjs:6-17](../lib/feed/taste.mjs)). This matches the industrial dual-rate pattern of Pinterest's homefeed, which combines a long-horizon user embedding (PinnerFormer) with a real-time sequence model (TransAct) ([arXiv 2209.08435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.08435); [arXiv 2205.04507](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.04507)). Keeping a separate vector per topic, "so two loves don't average into neither", is a lightweight version of multi-interest retrieval ([MIND](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.08030); [ComiRec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.09347)).

Ranking is equally mature. A batch of 10 is filled by slot quotas that shift from a cold mix (**60% explore**) to a warm mix (**50% core**). The core slot uses Thompson sampling over the interest pool, damped by fatigue. The explore slot uses optimistic upper-bound scoring, the adjacent slot uses authored topic "bridges", the fresh slot pulls the newest unseen items, and the similar slot pulls pgvector nearest neighbours. That similar share adapts to how well it has performed for the reader ([engine.mjs:7-17, 59-65, 356-371, 468-498](../lib/feed/engine.mjs)). An arranger then enforces "never the same horizontal back-to-back", **≤3 per topic in a 6-item window**, and fatigue `exp(−0.18·count in last 30)` ([engine.mjs:18-24, 499-555](../lib/feed/engine.mjs)). Every item carries a human-readable reason ("bridge from X to Y", "87% similar") ([engine.mjs:380-387](../lib/feed/engine.mjs)). These are the same mechanisms the literature recommends against degenerate feedback loops. DeepMind shows that **continuous random exploration** and a growing candidate pool are the best brakes on interest collapsing to an extreme ([Jiang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.10730)). Google measured **+1.25% more unique topics** with positive interactions and a stable rise in satisfied DAU from posterior-sampling exploration ([Su et al., WSDM 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.07764v2)).

The weakness lies in signal semantics. The UI emits only like, save, hide and a derived dwell/skip. Share and comment are accepted by the server but never sent, and Similar clicks, search clicks and Reddit clicks are not logged at all ([live.mjs:8](../lib/feed/live.mjs); [shorts_feed.jsx:197-220](../components/internal/screens/feed/shorts_feed.jsx); [use_live_feed.js:156-170](../components/internal/screens/feed/use_live_feed.js)). Explicit actions are deduplicated per item, so a like or hide cannot be undone ([use_live_feed.js:22-29](../components/internal/screens/feed/use_live_feed.js)). Each surviving event becomes a single success/weight pair. That pair spreads up the tree with weights of 1 for the horizontal, 0.9 for the step, 0.8 for the subtopic and **0.6 for the topic** ([engine.mjs:227-237](../lib/feed/engine.mjs)), and it also pushes the taste vectors ([taste.mjs:50-76](../lib/feed/taste.mjs)). Two consequences follow, and both conflict with the evidence. First, a single fast skip (`<1 s`, success 0, weight 0.8) lowers topic-level interest and pushes the negative vector. Kuaishou/WeChat research instead treats passive skips as evidence about the reader's *currently active sub-interest*, not about the topic as a whole ([Pan et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.04086)). Second, a dwell of 2.5 s or more scores success up to **0.85**, close to a like, whatever the reason the reader lingered. That is the arousal-blind pattern Berger & Milkman link to anger- and anxiety-driven sharing ([Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf)). The asymmetry between positive and negative signals is also mild. Hide carries 2.5 against 1.2 for a like. X's open-sourced ranker weighted "show less" at **−74** and report at **−369** against **0.5** for a like ([X the-algorithm-ml README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/twitter/the-algorithm-ml/main/projects/home/recap/README.md)). The two systems use different mechanics, so the numbers are not directly comparable, but the gap in intent is clear.

## Four reader states need four separate update paths

Interest, liking, emotional reaction and knowledge are distinct targets, and the literature already sorts them into separate machinery. **Interest** is "want more like this". Milli et al. validated exactly that wording ("do you want to be shown tweets like this?") as a stated-preference probe and found that it diverges from engagement ([arXiv 2305.16941](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.16941v6)). **Liking** corresponds to YouTube's "satisfaction objectives" (likes, ratings), which its multi-task ranker models separately from engagement objectives such as clicks and watch time ([Google Research, MMoE](https://research.google/pubs/recommending-what-video-to-watch-next-a-multitask-ranking-system/); [DSelect-k](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.03760)). **Emotion** is where engagement and value diverge most. High-arousal emotion predicts spread, both the positive kind (awe) and the negative kind (anger) ([Berger & Milkman](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf)). Meanwhile, Facebook's 2017 decision to weight every reaction at **5× a like** amplified anger until the angry weight went to zero ([Nieman Lab](https://niemanlab.org/2021/10/more-internal-documents-show-how-facebooks-algorithm-prioritized-anger-and-posts-that-triggered-it); [Platformer](https://platformer.news/the-facebook-papers-missing-piece)). **Knowledge** is almost absent from feed-ranking literature. Educational psychology treats it as co-developing with interest but separate from it ([Hidi & Renninger four-phase model](https://works.swarthmore.edu/fac-education/12)). Recommender work shows that experienced users value different items than novices ([McAuley & Leskovec](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.4402)). Kleinberg, Mullainathan and Raghavan formalise why this separation matters. A platform that observes only engagement will systematically optimise for the impulsive "in-the-moment" self over the reader's longer-horizon preferences ([arXiv 2202.11776](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.11776)).

The existing code splits cleanly along these lines with little rework. The **topic tree** already represents topic-level appetite with uncertainty, so it should become the *interest* store. The **taste vectors** are image embeddings that capture look, style and subject resemblance, so they are the natural *liking* store. Their "close to images you engage with" reason already describes liking, not topic intent. Two new stores complete the model. The first is a per-reader **emotion preference vector** over a handful of reaction buckets. The second is a per-(reader, subject) **knowledge ability θ**. Ranking then becomes a weighted sum of per-dimension scores. This is the same "predict each head, combine with a value model" pattern used by YouTube, by Tencent's PLE and by Kuaishou's learned fusion. Kuaishou's end-to-end ensemble reported **+1.39% stay time** ([arXiv 2508.05093](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.05093v2); [PLE](https://www.paperswithcode.com/paper/progressive-layered-extraction-ple-a-novel)). The final weights are exactly what an operator or reader adjusts ("more amazing", "less outrage", "more learning") without retraining anything.

### Proposed signal and reaction schema

Storage needs almost no schema work. `content.feed_events.type` has **no check constraint** and the table already carries a `metadata jsonb` column ([postgres/migrations/20261002234213_feed_embeddings.sql:61-75](../postgres/migrations/20261002234213_feed_embeddings.sql)). New types therefore only need adding to `EVENT_TYPES` and to `engagementSignal`, plus a change so that `logEvents` writes `metadata`, which today it drops ([vectors.mjs:175-183](../lib/feed/vectors.mjs)). The table below keeps the existing weights where a signal already exists. Weights for new signals are proposed starting points to tune in Feed Lab, not published values.

| Signal (event `type` / `metadata`) | Captured today? | Interest (topic tree) | Liking (taste vectors) | Emotion vector | Knowledge θ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `like` | Yes (1, 1.2) | + small (spread capped at subtopic) | + long-term and short-term channel | — | — |
| `save` | Yes (1, 1.6) | + strong (future utility) | + | — | + small ("want to learn") |
| `share` | Server only (1, 1.6) | + only when the paired emotion is non-negative | + | + bucket of attached reaction | — |
| `dwell` ≥2.5 s | Yes (≤0.85, 0.7) | + **only if no negative reaction on the item**; cap success at ~0.6 | + weak | — | — |
| `skip` <1 s | Yes (0, 0.8) | **session/sub-interest only**; topic penalty after ≥3 skips in the same subtopic | short-term channel only, not negative vector | — | + small if item is basic and subtopic affinity is high |
| `hide` + `reason` | Hide yes; reason no (0, 2.5) | `this_item`: horizontal only. `this_topic`: subtopic/topic strong negative. `seen_it`: **no change** | negative vector for `this_item` and `this_topic` | `upsetting`: negative on item's emotion bucket | `seen_it`: raise θ |
| `react` `{emotion}` | No | amused/amazed/moved: + mild. angry/disgusted: **0** | amused/amazed: + | update bucket preference | — |
| `knew_this` | No | **no change** | — | — | **raise θ**, demote basics |
| `learned` | No | + | + | — | + nudge θ toward item difficulty |
| `explore_click` (Similar/search result) | No | + mild on clicked item's subtopic | + (deliberate selection) | — | — |
| `outbound` (Reddit permalink) | No | + (deliberate, costly) | — | — | — |
| `probe_answer` `{more|same|less}` | No | strongest interest label; also calibrates other weights | — | — | — |
| `undo` `{of}` | No | reverse prior event | reverse | reverse | reverse |

Two rules carry most of the value. First, **negative-arousal engagement never raises interest**. Dwell, share or comment attached to an angry or disgusted reaction should update only the emotion vector, which is the Facebook and Milli lesson put into code. Second, **"seen it" and "knew this" never lower interest**. They move knowledge θ and shift the reader toward deeper or newer items in the same subject. This is the cleanest disambiguation available. Knowledge-depth research proposes it, and engagement-only rankers routinely get it wrong ([Pan et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.04086); [expertise reversal](https://unsworks.unsw.edu.au/entities/publication/2222917b-75b2-411f-b259-8a79dff5a22f/full)). Emotion should be inferred from both sides. On the item side, extend the Qwen captioning prompt in `scripts/kaggle/feed_enrich.py` to emit a tone/emotion label into `feed_image_captions.data`, a jsonb column the catalog does not even load today ([vectors.mjs:54-59](../lib/feed/vectors.mjs)). On the user side, record the explicit reaction tap. A practical bucket set collapses GoEmotions' 27 categories ([Google Research](https://www.research.google/blog/goemotions-a-dataset-for-fine-grained-emotion-classification/)) into about seven: amused, amazed, moved, curious, sad, angry and disgusted, each tagged with valence and arousal. Comment-text emotion classification can come later. The current UI has no comment box, and the "haha" reaction shows that amusement can be mocking rather than positive ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/emoji-reactions-cute-addition-facebook-became-headache-rcna3747)).

## Drift and rabbit holes are the same tree moving at two speeds

Drifting interest is already *absorbed* by the decay constants. It is not *detected*. Drifting personas such as "Gamer whose interest shifts to Formula 1" exist only in the simulator ([simulator.mjs:87-90, 160-165](../lib/feed/simulator.mjs)). Industry mostly handles drift through real-time training rather than explicit detectors ([Monolith](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07663)), but an operator-facing product benefits from labels it can show and act on. The research supports a simple classifier built on a fast and a slow rate per topic. An **emerging** interest is one where the fast rate stays well above the slow rate across several sessions. A **spike** exceeds the threshold within a single session only. A **fading** interest stays below the slow rate over a sustained window. A fading interest must not be confused with **satiation**. Rebounding-bandit theory models reward that drops with consecutive exposure and recovers with rest ([Leqi et al., NeurIPS 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.06741)). The engine already applies a fatigue term, so a topic that declined while fatigue was suppressing it should be labelled "rested", not "lost". Bayesian online change-point detection on the per-session topic distribution is the principled upgrade when a single regime-change flag is wanted ([Adams & MacKay](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742)). The research notes found no published thresholds, so θ and *k* (the number of sessions) are tuning parameters to calibrate against Feed Lab's drifting personas. Implementing this means keeping a second, fast Beta posterior per topic node, or deriving the fast rate from `feed_events`, which is stored precisely "so profiles can be replayed after engine changes".

Interest funnels are where customisation and safety meet. Audits show narrowing can be fast. In Amnesty's audit, accounts posing as 13-year-olds with an interest in mental health were served close to **half** mental-health content after about **5–6 hours**, roughly **10×** the volume served to accounts without that interest ([Amnesty UK](https://www.amnesty.org.uk/knowledge-hub/all-resources/report-driven-into-darkness/)). Post-2019 YouTube recommendations, by contrast, *moderated* partisan consumption relative to real users' own choices ([Hosseinmardi et al., PNAS 2024](https://css.engineering.upenn.edu/5466-2/)). The difference is domain: emotionally sticky themes narrow quickly. Platforms respond with repetition caps on themes that are harmless once and harmful in bulk. TikTok swaps out videos when sensitive themes repeat ([Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/tiktok-refresh-for-you-feed/482667)), and YouTube limits repeated body-image and social-aggression content for teens ([TechXplore/AFP](https://techxplore.com/news/2023-11-youtube-aim-teens-binging-body.amp)). Geiger's legacy "edgy" pool states that "Steps run mild → edgy, so depth doubles as intensity" ([taxonomy/edgy.mjs:1-3](../lib/feed/taxonomy/edgy.mjs)). That design conflates two axes that should stay separate. **Knowledge depth** is a desirable funnel, because readers want to go deeper. **Emotional intensity** is the hazardous funnel. The offering should therefore present "Rabbit-hole mode" as an opt-in that raises core share and probe rate along the *depth* axis, with three safeguards. The first is a descent-rate cap of a fixed number of levels per session. The second is a separate intensity tag from the emotion pipeline, with a sensitive-theme frequency cap that is stricter for minors. The third is the existing anti-circling arranger, which stays on as a floor. Funnel health then needs metrics the panel can show. The engine already computes `circlingIndex` and topic entropy but does not display them ([engine.mjs:557-588](../lib/feed/engine.mjs)). The research notes add depth-per-subject, topic churn, max run-length and exploration yield as proposed definitions. They are not standardised in the literature. Exploration changes should be judged on long-horizon metrics, because Google found standard A/B tests "often measure neutral or even negative engagement metrics" while missing exploration's long-term value ([Su et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.07764v2)).

## Knowledge depth is whitespace, and the frontier code is waiting for it

The engine already implements a depth funnel. `frontier[subtopic]` records the deepest step the reader has engaged with. A success of at least 0.6 on a deeper item advances it, misses set a cooldown, and "quiet probes one step deeper" are spaced at least four positions apart ([engine.mjs:25-33, 244-261, 445-461](../lib/feed/engine.mjs)). It does nothing in the live feed. The active `entertainment-v2` taxonomy projects every subject to a single step at depth 1, its validation forbids subjects from forming "an expertise ladder", and so the probe condition `frontier >= sub.maxDepth` always skips ([taxonomy/entertainment.mjs:1-3, 56-70](../lib/feed/taxonomy/entertainment.mjs); [taxonomy/index.mjs:98](../lib/feed/taxonomy/index.mjs)). That taxonomy decision is defensible, because content type (meme, analysis, highlight) is not expertise. The fix is therefore to give **items** a difficulty instead of turning the taxonomy back into a ladder.

The transferable model is Elo/IRT, not deep knowledge tracing. Duolingo's Birdbrain estimates learner ability and exercise difficulty with one online step per answer, which it describes as a generalisation of chess Elo, and targets "Goldilocks" difficulty ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/duolingo); [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/learning-how-to-help-you-learn-introducing-birdbrain)). Pelánek finds Elo "simple, robust and effective enough" for adaptive systems ([Pelánek 2016](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/CAE-elo.pdf)). Benchmarks show that simple baselines are tough to beat and that deep models struggle with cold start ([arXiv 2112.15072](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.15072); [EDM 2025](https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2025/proceedings/2025.EDM.industry-papers.46/2025.EDM.industry-papers.46.pdf)). The proposed build has four parts. The first is a per-item difficulty `b`, seeded by an LLM label added to the existing captioning pass. LLM-extracted Bloom levels already seed IRT difficulty in recent work ([arXiv 2602.00034](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.00034)). The second is a per-(reader, subject) ability θ, updated from *pseudo-outcomes*: "knew this" (strong), "learned something" (moderate), a quick skip of a basic item in a liked subject (weak), and an optional one-tap poll (strongest). The third is a target band in which predicted success σ(θ−b) sits near 0.70–0.85. That band is inspired by the "85% rule", which was derived for gradient learners and has not been validated for media ([Wilson et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6831579)). The fourth is a basics-suppression rule for high-θ readers, justified by expertise reversal. Rewatching should mark an item as hard rather than mark the reader as expert, because slower playback, pauses and rewatching track *perceived difficulty* ([EPFL MOOC study](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/207968?ln=en)). Self-reported level should be only a weak prior. In RecQuest, self-described novices outperformed self-described experts, and the best language-based expertise classifier reached just **0.38 macro-F1** across three levels ([arXiv 2512.13173](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13173v2)). For the reader-facing surface, Khan Academy's revisable Familiar/Proficient/Mastered labels are the clearest precedent ([Khan Academy](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/5548760867853)). The research notes found no short-form feed that exposes a per-topic depth control. That makes it a differentiator, and also an unvalidated one, so it should launch behind Feed Lab simulation and an opt-in.

## Prioritised offering list

The industry pattern has converged on four kinds of control. The first is editable inferred interests (TikTok topic sliders, Instagram "Your Algorithm" with immediate per-topic raise/lower) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/30/tiktoks-new-manage-topics-tool-gives-you-more-control-over-your-for-you-feed-heres-how-to-use-it); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/instagram-your-algorithm-465987)). The second is time-boxed natural-language steering (Threads "Dear Algo" lasts **3 days** with a cap of **6 active** requests) ([Meta newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/02/threads-dear-algo/)). The third is "reduce, not remove" content-class tuners (Pinterest's per-category GenAI tuner) ([Pinterest Newsroom](https://newsroom.pinterest.com/news/pinterest-rolls-out-new-tools-to-give-users-more-control-over-gen-ai-content/)). The fourth is reset and exclusion primitives (TikTok Refresh; Spotify's exclude-from-taste-profile) ([Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/tiktok-refresh-for-you-feed/482667); [Spotify Support](https://support.spotify.com/ly-en/article/exclude-playlists-or-tracks-from-your-taste-profile/)). In the EU, a non-profiled option is mandatory for very large platforms. A Dutch court ruled in 2025 that the choice must persist rather than revert ([The Record](https://therecord.media/dutch-court-meta-violated-european-law-social-feeds)). Most Geiger buyers will not be very large platforms, but a persistent setting is the safe default. Geiger's structural advantage is that `composeFeed` already accepts arbitrary config overrides ([engine.mjs:424-427](../lib/feed/engine.mjs)), and settings already persist on `state.settings` and survive reset ([vectors.mjs:185-189](../lib/feed/vectors.mjs)). Most of Tier 2 is therefore validation code and UI, not engine work.

| 21 | Time-boxed text steering ("Dear Algo") | **New build** | Prompt embedded via `query-text.mjs` into the shared Nomic space as a temporary taste channel with expiry and a cap on active requests | `live.mjs:181-185`; `taste.mjs` |
| 22 | Stated-interest probe | **New build** | Occasional "Want more like this?" card to calibrate weights against stated preference | new event `probe_answer` |
| 23 | Exclude-from-taste and scoped reset | **New build** | Mark a session or topic as "not me" (Spotify-style) or reset one topic | `feed_events` flag; `vectors.mjs` |
| 24 | Persistent non-profiled mode | **New build** | Fresh/popularity-only composition, persisted in settings | `composeFeed` config with interest/similar slots off |

Sequencing follows dependency and risk. Items 13–16 come first, because every later build needs clean, routable signals and a configurable weight table. Items 7–12 can ship in the same sprint as pure exposure work, validated by running Feed Lab personas at each preset. Item 17 should precede item 20, because a rabbit-hole mode without an intensity axis repeats the legacy "depth doubles as intensity" mistake. Item 18 is the differentiator but carries the most uncertainty. Its pseudo-outcomes, its 0.70–0.85 band and the value of "knew this" are all unvalidated for entertainment imagery, and the image-only Reddit corpus offers thinner depth cues than explainer video would.

## Conclusion

The most useful reframing is that Geiger does not need a new recommender. It needs to stop feeding four different questions through one scalar. The current code already carries two orthogonal stores: a topic tree that is naturally *interest* and embedding vectors that are naturally *liking*. Two more (emotion, knowledge) plus a routing table turn an engagement maximiser into something buyers can steer along named dimensions. That steering is the product. Industry has spent 2024–2026 converging on user-editable interest models and time-boxed steering, while Geiger has quietly accumulated about 40 tuned parameters behind a single recency switch.

The two highest-leverage bets are also the two least proven. Separating emotion is backed by strong negative evidence (outrage amplification) but has little positive A/B evidence on reaction-set design. Knowledge depth has solid foundations in education but has never been validated in an entertainment feed. Both should therefore ship as opt-in modes, measured on long-horizon metrics in Feed Lab and on `feed_events` replays rather than on short-term engagement, which, as Google showed, systematically undervalues the exploration and diversity these offerings exist to protect.
